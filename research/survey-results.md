# Qualitative Research · Host Survey, first responses

> A short Google Forms survey sent to small Spanish hosts I know, November 2026. **n = 3.** It's an early signal to validate, not a result. Anonymised: no names or contact details are kept in this repo. How it feeds the next steps: [`what-comes-next.md`](../08-roadmap/what-comes-next.md).

_____

## Who answered

All three are owners with **one home**, renting to tourists for **1–3 years**, mainly on **Airbnb** (one also on Booking.com), with **no management software**. Two homes are in Madrid and one in Comunitat Valenciana. One owner lives in a different city from the home. One **stopped renting** in the past 12 months.

_____

## Results

| What we tested | Result | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **Gate: legal issues ranked 1st or 2nd** (target ≥ 60%) | **1 of 3 (33%).** Only one ranked "regulation, licences and registries" first; nobody ranked "a platform hides or removes my listing" | Below the gate. The signal to watch |
| **Open question: biggest worry in the past 12 months** | Guest satisfaction and reviews · ranking on Airbnb · safety when receiving guests | No one mentioned legal issues unprompted |
| **Top-3 ranking** | Most chosen: problem guests and reviews (two 1st places), cleaning and operations, platform commissions | The felt pain is in guests and operations |
| **Legal events in the past 12 months** | **2 of 3 had a rule change** affecting their home; in one case **the building's community voted against tourist rental**. One had guest-registry problems | The risk is real… |
| **Confidence they operate legally today** (1–5) | 4, 4 and 5 | …but they don't feel at risk: a **latent pain** |
| **Guest registry** | One automated, one manual, one "I don't do it or I'm not sure" | One host is exposed without knowing it |
| **Usefulness** (1–5) | Alert before a rule changes: 5, 5, 1 · Same number on every platform: 5, 5 · Tourist vs seasonal in my region: 5, 5 · Documents with dates: 5, 4, 2 · Costs per home: 3, 5, 3 | The two current hosts rate the core features very highly |
| **Willingness to pay** (per home per month) | **2 of 3: €10–20**; 1: only if free | Promising, but too few answers to rely on |
| **Concerns about such a tool** | Wrong legal information (2) · too complicated (2) · price (1) | Human review and simplicity are non-negotiable |
| **"One thing for peace of mind with the legal side"** | "An insurance" · "An organisation like a consumer association to answer questions" | Hosts want protection and someone to ask, not just information |

_____

## What it changes

1. **Compliance looks like a latent pain, not a felt one.** Hosts value the solution once they see it, but it isn't what keeps them up at night. If this holds with more answers, the risk is building a "vitamin".
2. **Positioning:** lead with protecting income and peace of mind, with compliance as the engine underneath.
3. **The Aha must be a protected booking, not a status screen.**
4. **Possible bundle:** guest safety and "insurance" came up unprompted. Verified guests + automated guest registry + rule alerts could be the entry point.
5. **Human help matters:** "someone to ask" plus the fear of wrong information supports the human-approved [AI regulatory watch](../05-concept/ai-regulatory-watch.md).

_____

## Limitations

- **n = 3, a convenience sample** (hosts I know). No statistical weight.
- **All one-home owners on Airbnb:** says little about managers or multi-channel hosts.
- **One respondent left most of the usefulness grid blank.** Fix: require an answer per row.
- **Possible ambiguity:** two said they keep documents "with software" but use no management software. Reword that option to "an app or the cloud (Drive, Dropbox…)".

**Next:** keep the survey open to n ≥ 30 and run 8–10 interviews (Gate 1 in [`what-comes-next.md`](../08-roadmap/what-comes-next.md)).
