## 1 · The cost of inaction

**Hosts are not losing to fines. They are losing their permission to operate.**

| What happens today | What the host loses | What Hospitable loses |
|---|---|---|
| A rule changes in the region, the city or the building | The licence or the right to operate, sometimes retroactively | — |
| A platform checks the registration number and delists | Every booking on that channel until it's fixed | The listing stops being an **active property**, so it stops paying a subscription |
| A guest-registry entry is late | Exposure to a sanction | Trust in the platform |
| A neighbours' vote or a formal demand goes unnoticed | In the worst case, the use of the home for up to 3 years | The account |

**Illustrative cost for one listing** (to replace with real data in discovery): €100 a night at 70% occupancy is about **€2,100 a month** of revenue lost while a listing is delisted. For Hospitable, a listing that stops having check-ins stops counting as active: **$10–30 a month** per property on paid plans.

**The critical insight:** hosts don't *choose* to be non-compliant. They *find out* they are, because nothing shows them their status per listing before it's too late. It isn't a preference; it's a missing tool.

## 2 · Who we're building for

| Role | Who | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary buyer** | Small hosts in Spain with 1–5 listings, resident or non-resident ([persona](../07-validation/hypothesis.md)) | Keep earning legally, without surprises |
| **Secondary buyer** | Small managers (5–50 homes) who run homes for owners | Track every licence and application across owners and regions |
| **Influencers** | Gestorías, property administrators (*administradores de fincas*), host associations (Fevitur, Avaec, Arvurtur) | A reliable, dated record they can work from |
| **Affected parties** | Guests (smooth registry check-in), neighbours (a visible, compliant host), councils (fewer illegal listings) | — |

**Jobs to be done**
- **Functional:** know whether each listing can legally operate right now, and fix it before a platform or a council acts.
- **Emotional:** sleep at night; stop dreading the next rule change.
- **Social:** be seen as the legal, responsible neighbour, not the "tourist flat" that gets reported.

| | |
|---|---|

| **Who need** | To know, per listing, that they are legally allowed to operate right now, before a platform, a council or their neighbours decide for them |

### Positioning statement

> For small Spanish hosts who find out they're non-compliant only after the damage is done, **Hospitable Compliance Hub** is the one place that shows whether every listing can legally operate right now, across every channel. Unlike platform checks that see one listing at a time, or a gestoría that reacts after the fact, it warns hosts before a rule, a platform or a neighbours' vote takes the listing down.

## 4 · Messaging framework

**Core message:** *"Your licence. Every channel. Always current."*

**From → To:** from *"I find out when my listing is gone"* to *"I know before anything happens"*.

| Pillar | Capabilities | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **See it once, fixed everywhere** | Status per listing · registration number synced to every channel · lead-listing view across Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo, direct | No delisting for a missing or mismatched number |
| **Warned before, not after** | Alerts on draft and new rules by region and city · registry deadlines · community dispute steps and challenge deadlines | Time to act before the damage |
| **Proof when it counts** | Dated evidence vault · export for an inspection · proof of registration before April 2025 for the transitional rule | Keep the right to operate when dates decide |

**By persona**

| Persona | Headline |
|---|---|
| Resident small host | *"Know your listings are legal today, not after a platform tells you."* |
| Non-resident owner | *"Your Spanish rental, compliant from wherever you live."* (in English, German, French) |
| Small manager | *"Every licence, every owner, every region. One view."* |
| Gestoría / administrator (partner) | *"Your clients' paperwork, dated and in one place."* |

**Tone guardrails:** protection, not surveillance. Never "find a loophole". Never "you are protected" (we inform and keep evidence; we don't give legal advice).

## 6 · Pricing & packaging (proposal)

**The logic:** the tier a host chooses signals how much control they want. Compliance should pull hosts **up** from the free plan, not sit behind a paywall that leaves them exposed.

| Hospitable plan (current price) | Compliance Hub layer |
|---|---|
| **Essentials** · $0 | **Status view:** red / amber / green per listing and what's missing. Free, to remove activation friction. Upgrade prompt when an action is needed |
| **Host** · $29 + $10 per extra property | **Compliance core:** number sync and listing check on every channel, guest registry via partner integrations, rule alerts for the listing's region and city |
| **Professional** · $59 + $15 | Core + community status and dispute tracker · cost view (10% VAT, community and IBI surcharges) |
| **Mogul** · $99 + $30 | Everything + licence process tracker across owners · evidence export for inspections · multi-region view |

**Alternative to test:** a compliance add-on for Essentials users at a fixed fee per active property, following the Dynamic Pricing add-on model ($15 per property). Decide after the pricing interviews in discovery.

**Pricing metrics to watch:** Essentials → paid conversion among Spanish hosts; share of upgrades that start from a compliance prompt; churn of Spanish accounts.

## 7 · The flywheel

Compliance status → listings stay live → more active properties (subscription revenue) → more compliance data → **GoodHost Score** ([`05-concept`](../05-concept/goodhost-score.md)) → councils can cap by quality instead of banning everyone → hosts have a reason to be compliant through Hospitable → more hosts join.

**Why it doesn't fight the platforms:** Airbnb and Booking.com want compliant supply and are legally required to show registration numbers. Hospitable keeps that supply clean on every channel at once, which no single platform can do.
