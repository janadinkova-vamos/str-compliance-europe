# Qualitative Research · Small-Owner Voices (press)

> Testimonies of small Spanish owners (1–3 homes) and one small manager. **This is our core persona.** The operator interviews in [`interviews.md`](interviews.md) are outside it. Complements [`qualitative-reddit.md`](qualitative-reddit.md) and [`legal-communities.md`](legal-communities.md).

_These are testimonies collected by journalists, not our own interviews. They are directional evidence: the selection is the newspaper's and the speakers are self-selected. Paraphrased, with no long quotes._

_____

## Source 1 · El Mundo, 29 September 2025

Feature on small tourist-home owners after the national registry (NRUA) came into force.

| Who | Situation | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **Alejo Santolino**, A Coruña (non-resident, works in Moscow) | Had held a tourist licence for his attic in the historic centre since 2019, with regional (Xunta) approval. A new municipal rule, applied retroactively, limits tourist homes there to ground or first floors or whole buildings, so his licence is lost. He uses the flat when he's back and has left it **empty**. He won't rent it long term or sell it. | **He is the persona.** He lost his permission to operate through a local change he didn't see coming. The home does not return to the residential market; it stays empty. |
| **Antonio**, 72, Benidorm | Found the NRUA process unintuitive and some requirements absurd (three exit signs in a one-door flat). Rents to cover the €400–500 a month the flat costs him. Asked not to be photographed, for fear of stigma. | The process is painful, especially for older, less digital hosts. Stigma is real. |
| **Susana Díaz**, La Rioja (manages 43 homes; chairs the regional association Arvurtur) | Started with her parents' home. In Logroño she needs a tourist licence, a habitability certificate, a first-floor or mezzanine location and a 3/5 community vote. One licence waits on another, and **the rules change mid-process**, so files stall indefinitely. Supports regulation but fears more requirements in ten years. | Dependent licences and rules that move during the process: a manager pain that fits the Pro tier. |
| **José**, Córdoba (3 homes, IT worker) | Has his NRUA number. **95% of his bookings come from Airbnb and Booking.com.** Feels small owners are targeted while large companies buy whole buildings. Margins are lower than in 2020–21. | Channel dependence: syncing the registration number to every channel is critical. Also, a sense of unfairness versus large players. |
| **Rafael Encarnación**, lawyer (Avaec, Valencia) | Valencian rules date from 1993 and are read in conflicting ways. Owners who were compliant when they started are now pursued, left "in limbo". Councils lack reliable data. NRUA denials come from missing licences, community statutes and premises without a change of use. | Confirms fragmentation and the lack of reliable data. |

**Figures in the article** (September 2025, before the NRUA was annulled in May 2026):
- 271,724 NRUA applications for tourist homes: 189,515 granted (69.75%) and **57,295 denied (21.09%)**.
- INE counted 381,837 tourist homes in May 2025 (1.43% of housing). Andalucía had 96,176, and in Canarias tourist homes were 4.66% of housing.
- Compliant owners ask for a level playing field against illegal ones. Barcelona's tourist-apartment association runs an anonymous channel for reporting unlicensed flats.

## Source 2 · 20minutos, 28 September 2026

Explainer on how the Government's housing decree would affect owners of one or two homes. It was written while the text was still being negotiated, before it went to the Council of Ministers. It names four measures that would reach small owners:

1. **Extraordinary rental extension:** tenants whose contracts end before 30 June 2028 could ask for up to two more years on the same terms.
2. **Tax breaks** for owners who lower the rent.
3. **More control of seasonal and room rentals**, including private owners who let for months to students or posted workers.
4. **21% VAT on tourist homes** and stricter conditions for advertising them on platforms.

_Status to verify: a later headline on the same page says the Standing Committee of Congress saved the anti-eviction decree and the rental extension. Check which measures are in force before citing any of them._

## Implications for the product

**Confirmed, now with core-persona voices:**
- **The exact problem.** Alejo was fully licensed, and a retroactive local change took his permission away. Yesterday's legal is today's illegal, and the host doesn't control it.
- **Process friction, with data.** 21% of applications denied, a process older owners find unintuitive, and dependent licences that stall when rules change.
- **Channel dependence.** For José, losing a channel means losing the business.

**New for the solution:**
1. **Alerts on rules in progress, not just rules in force.** Alejo needed to know his council was drafting the rule. → Added to the Compliance Hub scope in the [roadmap](../08-roadmap/roadmap.md).
2. **Licence process tracker for managers.** Status of each application, its dependencies, and a flag when the rule changes mid-process (Susana). → Added to LATER as part of the Pro tier.
3. **Simple and discreet by default.** Plain Spanish, guided steps and human help for older hosts. Any public "good host" badge must be opt-in, given the fear of stigma.
4. **Mixed-use owners.** Several owners use the home themselves part of the year. If they lose the tourist licence, the home stays empty rather than going back to long-term renting. This supports the mid-term mode.
5. **Mid-term mode is now a regulatory target too.** The decree points at private seasonal renting, so that mode must be compliant from day one, not an escape route. → Added to the roadmap risk register.
