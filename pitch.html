A compliance-led strategy for short-term rentals in Spain: from market analysis to a tested hypothesis and a working prototype.

Jana Alexandrova

This deck walks through how we got from a broad question about the European short-term rental market to one testable bet and a working prototype: problem, data, hypothesis, solution, roadmap, prototype.

The problem

Spanish hosts find out they’re non-compliant only after the damage is done.

A delisted listing. A lost booking. A fine. Nothing shows them, per listing, whether they are legally allowed to operate right now.

Problem statement
The one-sentence problem we landed on after all the research. Note what it is not: it isn’t “regulation is strict”. It’s that hosts lack visibility and control over their permission to operate.

Each layer can stop a host from operating. Spain is the most volatile market in Europe; Portugal is calmer nationally but tightening city by city. The snap election on 29 November can change state levers (VAT, community and rental law), but regional licences and city rules stay whoever wins. Even the building layer varies: Catalonia has its own civil code, with a 4/5 majority to ban.

What the research changed

It’s not about complying with the law. It’s about who controls the permission to operate.

Regulation

Fragmented and slow

A non-resident buyer expects a licence to take 1–1.5 years and can’t tell which rules apply to his town.

Platforms

Rules change overnight

Hosts describe sudden policy changes, verification hurdles and delistings they can’t appeal.

Guests

Claims without evidence

“It was dirty” refund claims: hosts defend themselves with time-stamped photos, by hand.

“The rules and regs take far more time than you think.”

Veteran host, r/airbnb_hosts · Montgat buyer, r/askspain
This is the pivot point of the case. Three fronts can take a host’s permission to operate away. Compliance is the entry point we chose because it’s the confirmed #1 pain for our Spanish persona, and it opens the door to the other two later.

The critical insight: hosts don’t choose to be non-compliant, they find out they are. That isn’t a preference, it’s a missing tool. And because Hospitable bills per active property, every listing that falls offline is revenue Hospitable loses too. Compliance and revenue are the same metric.

Goal

Keep earning legally and predictably, without legal or platform surprises.

Friction (one)

No single, reliable view of whether each listing is legally allowed to operate right now.

Out of scope for now: large managers (10+ listings) and hosts outside Spain
Why call out non-residents? The research showed their pain is the most intense: distance, language, double tax filing. They already pay to solve the problem. We test with all small Spanish hosts and read non-residents as a subgroup.

Hypothesis

One testable bet

We believe that

small hosts in Spain with 1–5 listings lose bookings and peace of mind because they find out they’re non-compliant only after the damage is done.

If we

give them a compliance layer that validates their registration, syncs it to every channel, submits guest-registry data and alerts them before a listing is at risk,

Then

the share of their listings that are compliance-complete within 30 days will rise by at least 25%, without reducing listings that are live and bookable.

Because hosts don’t lack the will to comply; they lack visibility before the damage happens. Funnel stage: Retention (delistings end active properties) and Revenue (no trigger to upgrade from the free plan).

Evidence: 65,000+ Airbnb listings ordered removed (missing or wrong numbers, no host type) · 81,446 pending · 64,000 blocked
Built as a structured hypothesis: path, bet, evidence, persona and problem, then outcome and metrics. Note the guardrail is part of the hypothesis itself: we must not win by scaring hosts into unpublishing.

Defining the win, and the guardrail

How we’ll know it works

Strategic outcome

Fewer compliance-driven delistings, so lower 90-day churn of Spanish accounts.

Primary metric

+25%

relative lift in the 30-day compliance-complete rate

Guardrail

Share of listings live and bookable must not drop. Watch: time to first published listing.

Decision window

12 wk

8 weeks of enrolment plus a 30-day read

Scale: ≥ +25%, guardrail holds

Pivot: +10% to +25%

Kill: under +10%

Compliance-complete = valid regional number, synced to every channel, guest registry on time · baseline measured in week 0
Two corrections we made while defining this: the window had to grow from 8 to 12 weeks, because the metric is read 30 days after exposure; and the guardrail had to protect existing hosts, not just new ones. The +25% is the minimum lift worth building for, re-checked once we know the baseline.

The Compliance Hub

Compliance made visible, automatic and in the host’s control.

📊

Traffic-light status per listing from four checks: licence, channel sync, guest registry, building.

🔗

Listing check + sync

📨

Guest registry, via partners

Existing integrations (such as Chekin) file with SES.Hospedajes; the Hub shows proof per listing.

⚠️

Alerts before damage

Expiries, deadlines and new rules, each with a guided “Fix next” step.

📖

Plain-language rules

Partner-verified, timestamped, clearly not legal advice.

🔒

Host in control

We never unpublish a listing automatically. That is also our guardrail.

Guest registry is already covered by Hospitable partners: we activate it, we don’t rebuild it
The Aha moment: within 30 seconds, the host sees for every listing whether it can legally operate right now and what to fix first. Channel sync matters because the hardest case is a listing that is fine on one platform and missing its number on another.

The solution · AI regulatory watch

AI reads the bulletins. A person approves. The host sees only their change.

1 · Monitor

BOE, regional bulletins and main-city ordinances, every day

→

2 · AI drafts

Flags STR items, labels in force or being drafted, writes a plain summary

→

3 · Human approves

Legal partner approves, edits or rejects, within 48 h

→

4 · Host alert

Only listings the rule applies to: what, what to do, by when

Help, not burden

Per-listing, not a checklist for everyone · drafts and rules in force labelled apart · source and reviewer on every item · never slows the first listing

Measured by

Precision > 80% · source to alert ≤ 48 h · % of alerts acted on in time · guardrail: alerts per host capped

Neighbours’ votes are never published: the building layer comes from the host’s own documents
This is the engine behind “Alerts before damage” and “Plain-language rules”. Human approval is what makes it credible: a wrong legal alert is worse than none. The hardest part to scale is councils, so we start with the 5 MVP regions and their main cities.

Positioning

Why Hospitable, and what we’re not doing

Why Hospitable

  • Compliance is a cross-channel problem, and Hospitable already sits across Airbnb, Booking.com and Vrbo.
  • No single platform can own it: each only sees its own listing.
  • A host whose compliance runs through Hospitable has a strong reason to stay: compliance becomes retention.

Not doing

  • Giving legal advice: rules are partner-verified and clearly labelled.
  • Selling a way around regulation: the promise is “compliance made easy”.
  • Building an in-house tax engine: 10% VAT (live 1 Dec 2026) goes through invoicing partners.
  • Competing with the platforms on demand generation.
Positioning
The “not doing” list matters as much as the bet. It keeps us credible with platforms and regulators, and it keeps the first release small enough to test in one quarter.
